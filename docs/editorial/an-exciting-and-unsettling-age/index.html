NABLA EDITORIAL · 001

An Exciting and Unsettling Age

在一个令人兴奋，也令人不安的时代

创刊说明 / Founding note袁泳棋 / Yongqi Yuan18 October 2026

Abstract

Nabla Editorial begins as a record of how young researchers experience a changing world. Emerging from informal group discussions, this series explores research, technology, work and everyday life, with particular attention to the opportunities and uncertainties of artificial intelligence. It aims to preserve questions, disagreements and evolving perspectives, rather than predict the future.

English translation · 19 October 2026. 中文原文 first published 18 October 2026.

This is an age that is both exciting and unsettling. We want to keep a thoughtful record of what it means to live through it.

Beginning with Group Meetings

Nabla Editorial grew out of our regular group meetings. Alongside specific research questions, I wanted us to discuss research, technology, careers, money, society and personal life. Some thoughts arise from experience; others are simply observations about recent events. They are not always systematic or fully developed.

As a doctoral student working in computational fluid dynamics, fluid mechanics and related engineering research, I want to preserve these questions and reflections. They began as informal conversations within our team. Thinking about artificial intelligence and its social consequences, however, has gradually made me feel that such records might matter beyond our meetings.

Excitement and Unease

This is an age that is both exciting and unsettling.

Artificial intelligence is changing how we acquire knowledge, write code, analyse data and carry out scientific computation. New tools can help us complete many once time-consuming tasks more quickly. Research ideas previously constrained by individual skills, tools and resources may gradually become feasible. For young researchers, the range of things we can attempt is expanding. That is exciting.

Yet opportunity arrives with uncertainty. Will the knowledge and skills we invest in today retain their value? What abilities are several years of doctoral training really developing? As AI becomes better at reading papers, writing code, running simulations and analysing data, what remains at the heart of a human researcher's contribution?

These questions extend beyond research. Can greater efficiency lead to better lives? Can the benefits of technology be shared more widely? How will employment, education and research assessment change? There are no ready answers. What unsettles me is not only the technology itself, but also the pace of change and whether we can understand and adapt to it.

What Role Will We Play?

We may be living through a new industrial revolution driven by artificial intelligence. That is a possibility worth examining, rather than a certain conclusion about the future.

What excites us is the possibility that we are witnessing history; what unsettles us is that we do not know what role we will play in it.

We benefit from technology while also bearing its uncertainties. We welcome more efficient research, yet ask whether the meaning of research itself is changing. These tensions need not be resolved immediately. They are part of the experience of our time and deserve to be recorded.

Nabla Editorial therefore aims to remain open, rational and honest: attentive to new possibilities and serious about their problems, hopeful about the future and candid about our doubts.

Why Keep a Record?

People looking back on an era often already know its outcomes: which technologies succeeded, which predictions failed and which choices changed lives. Those living through it do not. An uncertain future is part of the experience of the present.

I am interested in how ordinary people, especially young researchers, understand and experience these changes. How does a doctoral student in 2026 see AI? How do they understand the value of research and their career prospects? How do they balance research, income, social relationships and everyday life?

Recording these reflections preserves more than conclusions. It also preserves the circumstances, expectations and limitations in which ideas formed. Today's judgments may later prove mistaken, but a thoughtful and honest record can still show how people understood the world at the time.

We need not pretend to predict the future, nor be right in every instalment. Sometimes a good unanswered question is worth writing down.

Letting Ideas Carry the Marks of Time

Nabla Editorial will preserve articles' first publication dates and original arguments. Later understanding can be added through new articles, dated postscripts or annotations. Factual or textual corrections should identify what changed and when, so that later knowledge does not silently overwrite earlier judgments.

We want to record processes, not just conclusions. Disagreements and questions that produce no consensus also deserve attention. Before quoting another member publicly, we should obtain their consent. Where appropriate, we should anonymise their remarks and confirm that the proposed anonymity respects their wishes.

All Nabla members may contribute signed articles. Each author retains their own voice. Articles represent the named author's thinking at the time of publication; they need not express a shared position of the team.

Writing for the Future, and for the Present

Beginning with research and everyday life, Nabla Editorial will continue to discuss technology, careers, money, society and our relationship with the times we live in. It has no predetermined set of complete answers and promises no fixed publication schedule. We hope to leave a thoughtful record when there is something worth considering.

Perhaps, years from now, these writings will offer a small but authentic glimpse of the history of a new industrial revolution. Even if they do not, they will record how we asked questions, exchanged ideas and sought our place amid change.

That is why we are beginning to write.
